# What a good SoapCraft article looks like

A SoapCraft article is a **decision surface**, not an SEO-shaped explanation. It earns its route only if the reader can say: *this named the decision I was stuck on, showed what changes the answer, made its assumptions visible, and gave me the shortest next step to use it on my batch.*

## The required brief

Before prose, a package records: reader situation; decision; primary question; live tension; primary intent; evidence available; limitations; one matching free tool CTA; and the original value this page adds.

A topic is not enough. “Pricing for soapmakers” fails. “A maker knows ingredient spend but cannot tell whether 36 saleable bars cover labels, market fees, labor, and the table” is a real decision.

## Required editorial moves

1. Start at the decision, with a concrete scene, contradiction, or consequence — never a dictionary definition.
2. Give a bounded answer near the top.
3. Explain the few inputs that change the answer and why they matter.
4. Work one reproducible example with explicit inputs, method, and assumptions. Label illustrative inputs as illustrative.
5. Separate known values, estimates, and reader choices.
6. Correct one tempting shortcut without mockery.
7. Explain limits and route the reader to the correct tool or authority for what the article cannot decide.
8. Earn one free tool bridge after the reader understands why it removes the next piece of work.

## Creative process before polish

The writer creates three materially different premises in the package, then records the selection:

- **Invisible cost:** ingredient spend is visible but yield, packaging, fees, and time are missing from the sellable-bar decision.
- **Target first:** a market table or wholesale order creates a revenue target that determines batch count backwards.
- **False certainty:** a universal multiplier feels decisive until it is tested against actual assumptions.

A premise changes why the reader cares; it is not a headline rewrite.

## Publishability

A meaningful search page normally needs 1,500+ rendered words because it needs an answer, reasoning, evidence, a worked example, limits, and a relevant next action. The word count is a floor, not proof of depth.

Reject a draft if it could serve any craft with noun swaps; cannot reproduce its example; makes unsupported safety, price, legal, customer, product, or performance claims; appends a generic CTA; or repeats its introduction instead of leaving the reader with a usable model.

## Independent review rubric

Score decision specificity, original value, evidence, reproducible reasoning, voice, product bridge, and useful boundaries from 0–3. Any consequential evidence or safety failure rejects the piece. GREEN requires at least 15/21, no zero, two mapped sources, an independent review, desktop/mobile/logged-out QA, and a passing release manifest. AMBER additionally requires independent evidence review. RED is never AI-only published.
